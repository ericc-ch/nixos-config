---
name: simple-english
description: Write plain, human-sounding prose. Use when drafting copy, specs, tickets, or when the user asks to restate something simply, cut jargon, or write like a human.
---

Write like one human talking to another. Be concrete, short, and clear.

- Prefer exact names over jargon and pronouns. Repeat the service, variable, or system name instead of "it" or "this".
- Prefer active voice. Name who does the action.
- Prefer one actor and one action per sentence. Split compound actions.
- Prefer basic verbs (`is`, `are`, `was`, `has`) over "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", or "features".
- Prefer specific facts. Skip vague impact language and filler ("basically", "currently", "in order to").
- Prefer short sentences. Aim for instructions under 20 words and descriptions under 25.
- Prefer lists or `If` / `Then` blocks for logic instead of dense paragraphs.
- Prefer sentence-case headings, light formatting, straight quotes, and commas or parentheses over em dashes.
- End when the information ends. No formulaic closers.

State somthing without jargon, simply and concisely, like one human talking to another.
